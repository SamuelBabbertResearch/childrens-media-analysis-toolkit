# Adult Perceptions of Pacing in Children’s Television

## Study Aims and Stimulus Criteria

**Working protocol for Samuel and Mia - Cedarville University undergraduate psychology study**  
**Current design:** Adult-only perceived-pacing study using the Option 3.5
Replicated Feature stimulus design  
**Status:** Season One-only, two-wave human-in-the-loop selection plan; not a
substitute for faculty, methods, IRB, or statistical review

The participant redesign was adopted on 2026-08-31. See
[STUDY_ADULT_ONLY_REDESIGN_DECISION_2026-08-31.md](STUDY_ADULT_ONLY_REDESIGN_DECISION_2026-08-31.md).

## The study in one sentence

We are studying how adults perceive the pace of short *Curious George* clips
and how those ratings relate to cut rate, visual motion, and audio intensity.

## Why this question matters

Pacing is a subjective judgment that may reflect several formal media
characteristics. CMAT supplies objective descriptions of the stimuli so that
adult pacing ratings can be examined in relation to cuts, visual motion, and
audio intensity. Adults report only their own experience; they are not asked to
serve as proxies for children.

The adult-only design was chosen because recruiting children would add parental
permission, child assent, safeguarding, and scheduling requirements that could
make the project infeasible within the available timeline. Asking adults to
predict children's responses would not solve that measurement problem: without
child ratings, the predictions could not be tested as accurate accounts of
children's perceptions.

The study is about **perceived pace**. It is not a test of whether a program is
overstimulating, harmful, healthy, educational, or developmentally appropriate.

## Research questions

### Primary question

How are measured cut rate, visual motion, and audio intensity associated with
adults' perceived-pacing ratings for selected *Curious George* clips?

### Secondary question

Within matched clip contrasts selected to vary primarily in one feature, does
the higher-feature clip receive a different adult pacing rating for:

- cuts per minute;
- visual motion; or
- audio intensity?

### Exploratory questions

- Do two independent contrasts for the same feature show similar rating patterns?
- How do the three measured features jointly relate to adult pacing judgments
  within this deliberately selected clip set?
- Do CMAT measurements provide a practical and reproducible way to locate,
  describe, and freeze stimuli for a human-participant study?

Feature-level interpretation is exploratory. Two pairs per feature cannot
establish the independent or general importance of cuts, motion, or sound.

## Planned participants and response task

Participants will be adults age 18 or older. The final recruitment source,
sample-size target, and any additional eligibility criteria must be specified in
the IRB application before recruitment begins.

After every 30-second clip, participants answer:

> How fast did this video feel?

The response scale is:

1. Very slow
2. Slow
3. In between
4. Fast
5. Very fast

Each adult watches every clip once and provides one rating of how fast the clip
felt to them. Participants are not asked how a child might perceive the clip.
Clip order must be randomized or counterbalanced according to a plan frozen
before data collection. All participants see both members of all six analytical
pairs, but the pairs are not identified or deliberately grouped during
presentation.

## Why use *Curious George*

Using one program holds many production characteristics more constant than a
cross-program comparison would. The clips share characters, visual style,
general production context, and intended format. This improves control while
searching for naturally occurring differences in cuts, motion, and sound.

The authoritative stimulus pool is the 30-file HD Season One corpus. Season Two
was also measured as a feasibility check, but it is excluded from stimulus
selection. Allowing cross-season pairs improved numerical matching primarily by
pairing different seasons, which would confound a target-feature contrast with
narrator and other season-level production differences. Requiring same-season
pair members produced negligible improvement over Season One alone, while
alternating narrator voices could add sequential rating context. Season Two
outputs are retained as an exploratory record, not mixed into the study pool.

The tradeoff is limited generalizability. Results from one season of one series
cannot automatically be generalized to other programs, genres, production
styles, narrators, seasons, or child age groups.

## The required stimulus set

The final study uses **12 unique, 30-second clips** arranged into **six matched
analytical pairs**. Participants rate every clip individually. They do not make
a forced-choice decision between the two clips in a pair; the pairing is an
analysis and stimulus-selection structure.

| Pair | Lower/comparison clip | Higher/feature clip | Selection objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| CUTS 1 | Clip A1 - lower cuts | Clip B1 - higher cuts | Motion and audio intensity as similar as possible |
| CUTS 2 | Clip A2 - lower cuts | Clip B2 - higher cuts | Independent replication with different scenes |
| MOTION 1 | Clip C1-L - lower motion | Clip C1-H - higher motion | Cuts and audio intensity as similar as possible |
| MOTION 2 | Clip C2-L - lower motion | Clip C2-H - higher motion | Independent replication with different scenes |
| AUDIO 1 | Clip D1-L - lower audio intensity | Clip D1-H - higher audio intensity | Cuts and motion as similar as possible |
| AUDIO 2 | Clip D2-L - lower audio intensity | Clip D2-H - higher audio intensity | Independent replication with different scenes |

No clip is reused in another pair. There is no common baseline repeatedly shown
to participants.

## What CMAT is looking for

CMAT divides the complete candidate pool into relative thirds for each primary
measure:

- **Low:** bottom third of the measured candidate distribution;
- **Middle:** middle third;
- **High:** top third.

Ties at a boundary remain tied. These labels describe this *Curious George*
candidate pool, not an absolute or clinical definition of low or high.

### Primary target: matched feature contrasts

For each feature, we want a large low-to-high difference in the target feature
and the smallest practical differences in the other two features. CMAT ranks
pairs using percentile separation so measures with different units are not
treated as if their raw scales were interchangeable.

The matching priorities are:

1. a clear separation in the target feature;
2. close matching on the two control features;
3. two independent pairs for each feature;
4. 12 unique clips with no clip reuse;
5. different source episodes within a pair;
6. normally no more than two selected clips from one episode;
7. scenes that remain defensible after human review.

### Secondary target: informative feature profiles

Profiles such as the following are useful for understanding whether the season
contains enough variation:

- high cuts / low motion / low audio intensity;
- low cuts / high motion / low audio intensity;
- low cuts / low motion / high audio intensity;
- low cuts / low motion / low audio intensity; and
- high cuts / high motion / high audio intensity.

The rare off-diagonal profiles are especially informative because cuts and
motion often rise together. However, Option 3.5 does **not** require every
possible high/low combination. A strong matched contrast can be useful even if
one or both clips contain a middle-level control feature.

## Why these three measures

### Cuts per minute

Cuts describe editing frequency. A clip with more true hard cuts per minute may
feel faster even when activity within each shot is similar. CMAT's automated cut
detector screens the full corpus and identifies promising candidates, but the
adjudicated manual hard-cut count is authoritative for final stimulus selection
and reporting. Automated and manual values are retained separately.

### Visual motion

Motion describes frame-to-frame visual change within and across the sampled
frames. A clip can contain substantial movement even without frequent editing,
which is why motion must be considered separately from cuts.

### Audio intensity

CMAT currently uses linear RMS. This is an objective signal-amplitude measure,
not perceptual loudness and not LUFS. It must be called **audio intensity** in
study materials and reporting.

Two clips can share a similar mean RMS while having different dynamics, music,
speech, or sound effects. Human review therefore remains necessary.

## Candidate-pool rules

- Use contiguous 30-second windows.
- Exclude the first **51 seconds** and the last **38 seconds** of every episode.
  These are the finalized opening and closing exclusions for this study and
  must be applied consistently to every source episode.
- Do not include the final shorter remainder unless the protocol explicitly
  changes from 30-second stimuli.
- Preserve absolute source timecodes for review and reproducibility.
- Use one source set consistently. Do not mix HD and SD measurements in one
  candidate distribution.
- Measure the same files participants will see. If finalist clips are
  transcoded, re-measure the exported participant-facing files.
- Use the saved **Adult Prediction of Children's Perceived Media Pacing -
  Feature Extraction** Version 1 recipe for the baseline candidate-pool run and
  Wave 1 comparison. This is a legacy, hash-bound recipe name retained for
  provenance; it does not describe the current participant design. Preserve its
  pinned settings and content hash unchanged.
- After Wave 1 manual coding, create one documented Version 2 recipe by changing
  only the cut-detection operationalization. Motion, shared frame sampling, and
  audio settings remain pinned to their Version 1 values. Record both recipe
  citations, content hashes, and the reason for the cut-method change.
- Freeze Version 2 before remeasuring the complete Season One pool for Wave 2.
  Do not retune after examining Wave 2 manual coding.
- Never overwrite automated candidate values with human values. Store the
  manual hard-cut overlay, coder provenance, and adjudication state separately.

The local HD and SD seasons are not identical masters: episode durations,
audio gain, and some measured cut counts differ. SD may be faster to process,
but SD timecodes and measurements cannot be treated as substitutes for HD.

## Human-in-the-loop measurement and selection plan

The study's settled methodological summary is:

> CMAT reproducibly screened a large video corpus and identified promising
> stimulus candidates. Automated cut detection was calibrated using hand-coded
> candidate clips, then audited on newly selected clips. Final stimulus
> selection used manually verified cut counts alongside automated motion and
> audio-intensity measurements.

This plan contains one bounded calibration cycle:

1. **Wave 1 calibration:** Freeze the current 12-clip Version 1 proposal and
   manually code true hard-cut timestamps and types without viewing CMAT's
   detections. Compare Version 1 against the manual reference.
2. **Version 2 calibration:** Apply one pre-documented show- and task-specific
   cut-detector calibration using Wave 1. Preserve Version 1 and save Version 2
   with a new recipe citation, content hash, and written reason. Do not alter
   motion or audio measurement.
3. **Wave 2 prospective audit:** Remeasure all 30 Season One HD files with frozen
   Version 2, generate new candidates, and manually code a new shortlist. Run
   both Version 1 and Version 2 against the same Wave 2 manual reference. Do not
   tune again after viewing Wave 2.
4. **Final constrained selection:** Select the final six pairs from defensible
   hand-coded Wave 1 and Wave 2 candidates. Use manual hard-cut counts wherever
   cuts are a target or control; use frozen automated motion and linear RMS for
   their respective dimensions; and apply the existing uniqueness, episode-
   diversity, feature-separation, control-matching, and human-content rules.
5. **Exact-file verification:** Export the approved 12 clips, remeasure the
   participant-facing files, and manually verify their final hard-cut counts so
   transitions at clip boundaries are treated consistently.

Wave 1 performance is calibration-set performance. Wave 2 is a prospective
audit of new clips produced by the intended search workflow. Neither is a
representative validation sample for every Season One scene or a validation of
CMAT as a whole. Pearson's `r` may be reported for automated versus manual cut
counts, but it must be accompanied by count-error and boundary-detection results
because correlation alone cannot reveal systematic overcounting or
undercounting.

## Human review requirements

CMAT proposes numerical matches; Samuel and Mia make the final stimulus
decision. Every Wave 1 and Wave 2 clip considered for final selection must be
watched in full and have its cuts manually coded under the frozen rules.

Reject or replace a clip when it contains:

- an opening, closing, title card, credits, station mark, or repeated bumper;
- a technical defect, damaged audio, unexpected silence, or decoding problem;
- a clip boundary that begins or ends at a confusing or unusually abrupt point;
- content that depends heavily on context outside the 30-second window;
- unusually distinctive dialogue, music, narrative tension, comedy, or visual
  novelty that makes the proposed pair obviously incomparable;
- substantially repeated footage from another selected clip;
- content that raises participant-welfare or IRB concerns; or
- another salient difference likely to overwhelm the intended feature contrast.

Prefer clips that:

- form a coherent, watchable 30-second segment;
- are understandable without extensive prior context;
- have clean audio and video throughout;
- represent different scenes and preferably different episodes;
- preserve the intended target-feature contrast after export and remeasurement;
- are reasonably similar to their pair partner on obvious narrative and formal
  properties not measured by CMAT.

Human review and verified cut counts do not turn naturally occurring clips into
an experiment that manipulates only one feature. They improve the defensibility
of the matched comparison while leaving the interpretation associational.

## Current feasibility evidence

As of August 17, 2026, the complete Version 1 HD Season One run contains 30
source files, 1,320 eligible contiguous 30-second windows, zero failed files,
six proposed matched pairs, and 12 proposed Wave 1 clips. The diversity guard
of at most two selected clips per source episode was not relaxed. The Version 1
measurement fingerprint is `a5714394da4d`.

Version 1 Season One thresholds are:

| Feature | Bottom-third maximum | Top-third minimum |
|---|---:|---:|
| Cuts | 14 cuts/min | 18 cuts/min |
| Motion | 0.05837 mean frame difference | 0.07300 |
| Audio intensity | 0.03775 RMS | 0.03963 RMS |

The current numerical proposal demonstrates that the pool contains enough
variation for two candidate contrasts per feature. It is not a finalist set.
Its twelve clips constitute Wave 1 calibration material until their content and
cuts have been reviewed manually.

Season Two was measured separately on August 18, 2026 and produced 880 windows
from 20 HD files with no failures. It is excluded from the authoritative pool
because its negligible same-season matching benefit did not justify introducing
narrator and season context into the participant task.

## What would count as a successful final stimulus set

Before building the participant task, the final set should satisfy all of the
following:

- 12 unique, exactly identified participant-facing files;
- two defensible matched contrasts for cuts;
- two defensible matched contrasts for motion;
- two defensible matched contrasts for audio intensity;
- no repeated common baseline;
- no clip reuse across pairs;
- acceptable human review and manual hard-cut verification of every clip and
  pair;
- manual cuts used for final cut contrasts and cut-control matching;
- frozen automated motion and audio-intensity values;
- Version 1 calibration results and a frozen Version 2 prospective Wave 2 audit;
- automated and manual cut results preserved side by side with provenance;
- CMAT remeasurement and manual cut verification of the exact exported files;
- a frozen manifest containing sources, absolute source timecodes, exclusions,
  measurements, feature labels, pair assignments, software configuration, and
  file identities;
- a frozen participant order/counterbalancing plan; and
- faculty/methods and IRB approval of the final stimuli and procedure.

## What the study can claim

The study may report:

- associations between the manually verified cut measure, the frozen automated
  motion and audio-intensity measures, and adult ratings within this clip set;
- adult rating differences across the six matched contrasts;
- whether similar patterns appeared in both pairs representing a feature;
- whether CMAT provided a usable, reproducible human-in-the-loop method for
  finding and characterizing stimuli for this study; and
- how Version 1 and the show-specific Version 2 cut detector agreed with manual
  coding on the explicitly labeled Wave 1 calibration and Wave 2 audit clips.

## What the study cannot claim

The study cannot establish:

- that cuts, motion, or audio intensity caused a pacing judgment;
- the independent or general importance of any feature across children's media;
- effects on attention, cognition, arousal, behavior, or development;
- whether a program is overstimulating, harmful, healthy, educational, or
  appropriate;
- how children perceive the clips or how a particular child will respond;
- that findings generalize beyond the selected clips, sampled participants,
  *Curious George*, or the adult recruitment population;
- that Version 2 is validated across every Season One scene merely because it
  improved on the task-selected Wave 2 clips; or
- that one study validates CMAT as a whole, including every measure, detector,
  recipe, construct, or composite in the software.
